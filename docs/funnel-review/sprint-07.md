## Findings

Read every figure below as a story about one day. The only piece in the corpus,
`build-your-first-ai-app`, was public for about one day of a 14-day window (released in both editions
on 2026-10-30). The LinkedIn figures are cumulative over about 18 hours from posting, and part of that
time is after the site window closed. Every site figure counts consenting sessions only.

### Finding 1 — The English edition got no measured readers. All the depth was in Portuguese

**What the numbers show.** The English article page recorded 0 views
(`views_path_en_blog_build-your-first-ai-app = 0`, n=23 page views). The neutral share address,
which carries no language, recorded 1 (`views_path_neutral_blog_build-your-first-ai-app = 1`, n=23).
The English edition logged no reading progress
(`article_progress_events:build-your-first-ai-app:en = 0`, n=12 progress events). The Portuguese
edition logged all of it: 12 of 12 progress events, from 2 users (n=2). One of those 2 users reached
75% and the end (`article_progress_75_users` = 1, `article_end_reached_users` = 1, n=2). On the post
side, the LinkedIn post reached 126 members (n=200 impressions) and got 1 link visit (n=126). That is
the step where reach stopped turning into visits.

**What it costs.** An English edition with no measured reader cannot show whether it reads at all,
and its readers can only arrive through the posts and share links that point at it. The language split
also carries the caveat the script prints: a link pinned to one language sets that value by the link,
not by the reader. So "0 English readers" may mean "0 visitors arrived through an English link". It
does not show that English-speaking readers turned the piece down.

**The change proposed, or the price of leaving it.** No content change. The next collection should
record which address each published post carried and the landing page of each LinkedIn session. The
3 LinkedIn-channel sessions (n=18) cannot be matched to the 1 post link visit (n=126) without that,
and the windows differ. Leaving it costs one more period where the English edition is invisible and
nobody can tell whether the cause is distribution or the piece.

### Finding 2 — Preview reads are in the external figures, and the internal-traffic marking removed nothing

**What the numbers show.** The held draft could be reached only through preview, under the retired
slug. It recorded 4 page views
(`views_path_pt_blog_seu-agente-diz-que-a-conta-fecha = 4`, n=23) and 2 reading-progress events
(`article_progress_events_retired_slug:build-your-first-ai-app:pt = 2`, n=12). Over the same window
the internal-traffic marking caught 0 sessions (n=18) and 0 events (n=77). In sprint-06 it caught
1 session (n=4) and 13 events (n=21).

**What it costs.** Preview is an owner-and-review surface. Reads there that the marking did not catch
mean the "external" figures can include the owner's own sessions, and the report header's line that
every all-users figure equals the external value only holds as far as the marking does. On a
Portuguese depth figure of 2 users (n=2), a single unmarked internal reader is half of it. Who read
the preview is not observable, so this is a question to answer, not a conclusion.

**The change proposed, or the price of leaving it.** The owner checks whether the devices and networks
he previews from are covered by the "Internal Traffic" marking. Until they are, read every
retired-slug and preview figure as probably internal, and keep it out of any reader-facing conclusion.
Leaving it costs every per-piece depth figure being an upper bound that cannot be separated from his
own reading.

No candidate rule is proposed. Neither finding is about how a piece is written, and no `figure:` line
in this collection supports a change to `published-voice`.

## What I would leave alone

- **Do not read sessions 3 → 18 as growth.** The sprint-06 window was 7 days and this one is 14. This
  window holds a release and the posts that announced it, and sprint-06's figure was net of internal
  traffic the marking caught (1 of n=4), while this one's marking caught nothing (Finding 2). The two
  periods are not alike enough to compare as a rate.
- **Do not pick a channel.** Direct 8, Instagram 3, LinkedIn 3, WhatsApp 3, X 1 (n=18) is too small
  a sample to support a channel decision, as in sprint-06.
- **The broken internal link is the sweep's finding, not this rite's.** `docs/iteration-sweep/sprint-07.md`
  records that the new piece's link to the earlier piece is double-prefixed in both locales and lands
  on the home page. The funnel cannot say whether anyone clicked it: `event_click = 2` and
  `event_outbound_click = 2` (n=77) carry no link target in this collection. The one reader who
  reached the end (n=2) is the kind of reader that link exists for, which is a reason to fix it. It is
  not a measured cost.
- **Contact stayed at zero** (`event_contact_click = 0`, `event_contact_reach = 0`, n=77; also 0 in
  sprint-06, n=8). With one day of public exposure, that is not yet a signal.
