### Citable findings

1. **The credit line and the body disagree about who wrote the parody, and the piece's whole ethical
   spine rests on that one fact.** — clause: *"if you cannot point to where in the source material (an
   ADR, a `CLAUDE.md` passage, a transcript, a prior published piece, an explicit answer he gave) a
   claim, a number or a stance comes from, it does not go in the draft as his."*

   The attribution block, above the fold, reads **"Everything I wrote is mine."** Two screens later
   the body says **"What came back was a full parody, in his shape"** and **"The model supplied the
   meter"** — an output, not an authorship. Then movement 3 says **"reproducing somebody else's form
   with *my substitutions* in it"**, which asserts he made them, and the close calls the same object
   **"a piece of machine-generated nonsense"**. Four sentences, three different authors. Issue #527
   calls it *"a parody the owner wrote"*; the draft's own account of the method says it came back from
   a prompt. I cannot point at a source that settles it, so it cannot stand in the draft as his.

   *What it costs the reader:* this is the one page on the site whose argument is that the borrowing is
   named honestly. A reader who notices the wobble does not conclude "ambiguous phrasing" — they
   conclude the attribution paragraph was written to look complete rather than to be complete, which is
   the exact reading the paragraph exists to prevent.

   *Smallest change that clears it:* one sentence in the credit block that says which is true — the
   words are the model's and the material is his, or he wrote the substitutions himself and the model
   supplied the meter — and then make movement 3 and the close use that same verb. No new claim, one
   decision. If the answer is not in the source material, it is a question back to him, not an
   inference.

2. **"The scope that got cut" prices his most disciplined work as the least embarrassing thing about
   the platform.** — clause: *"**Self-deprecation is not his signature and must not be written as
   one** — a draft that has him minimising his own work or hedging something he earned reproduces the
   thing he is working against."*

   Two sentences bracket that section: **"it is the part I am least embarrassed by"** and **"I am not
   going to pretend cutting is heroic."** Between them sit seven reversed decisions with numbered
   records, nineteen personas down to seven, sixty-nine skills down to fourteen — each with a date and
   a reason. The prose frames a run of documented, deliberate engineering as the least shameful item
   in a shameful set, and then pre-emptively denies it is worth anything. The same clause's companion
   is *"Describe what exists at the size it exists — neither inflated nor shrunk"*; this is shrunk.

   *What it costs the reader:* the section that most directly earns the article's thesis is the one
   the reader is told not to be impressed by, so they aren't.

   *Smallest change that clears it:* cut both sentences. What is left — the list, the counts, and
   *"It is what happens when you finally ask a component what it is for here, and it has no answer"* —
   is the plain description, at size, with no hedge on either side.

3. **The shipping-blind line is defended with a disclaimer where it needs a fact.** — clause: *"**Where
   a passage risks reading as a verdict on someone, concrete facts about what he was doing dissolve it;
   careful adjectives do not**"*.

   The tense argument itself is the right move and is honestly flagged. But what carries it is
   **"That state is the profession's, not a verdict on anybody in it, and certainly not on the people
   I did that work beside."** That is the draft telling the reader how not to read the line. The
   clause says a denial does not dissolve a verdict — a concrete fact about what he was doing does.
   **"mine included, for a long stretch of it"** gestures at one and supplies none.

   The article already contains the model, two sections earlier: *"same integration problems, same
   organisational shapes, same solutions, and technical growth I could no longer feel."* Concrete,
   first person, accuses nobody, needs no disclaimer after it.

   *What it costs the reader:* four paragraphs defending one line reads as a save, and the disclaimer
   is the sentence that makes it read that way. This is the piece's most exposed point and the defence
   is currently its weakest paragraph.

   *Smallest change that clears it:* replace the disclaimer with one sourced sentence about what
   shipping without gates actually looked like in his own work — a deploy he could not see the effect
   of, a change that went out with nothing checking it — and delete the "not a verdict on anybody"
   clause entirely. If no such fact is in the source, that is a question back to him, and the honest
   interim is to state the tense and stop, without the denial.

4. **Four passages exist to establish that he is publishing this for the right reasons.** — clause:
   *"**If a passage only earns its place because it positions him, it does not earn its place.**"*

   - Movement 1: *"I want to be exact about why I am publishing anything at all, because 'I made an AI
     write a funny thing' is not a reason."*
   - Movement 2: *"I am showing you the prompt because it is the method, not because it is evidence …
     and I would not waste your time on it."*
   - Movement 3: *"I would rather lose the joke than have the one page on this site that is playing
     loose."*
   - Movement 4 heading run-in: *"none of it should need a tool open to follow."*

   The third is the clearest case and the one to cut first: the two sentences before it already give
   the complete reason the parody is not printed (*"it is that song with the words swapped, line for
   line"* / *"not a thing this platform should publish"*). What the third adds is a preference ordering
   about himself. The first two are the same move at the level of the article's legitimacy — the piece
   pleads its own case three times before any of its content arrives.

   *What it costs the reader:* the reader is asked to admire the standard before being shown anything
   that meets it, and the audit — which meets it fourteen times — arrives after the reader has already
   been told twice how rigorous it will be.

   *Smallest change that clears it:* delete the movement-3 sentence outright; keep **one** of the two
   legitimacy statements (movement 2's is the load-bearing one, because it defuses the circularity)
   and cut the other. Net: three sentences out, no argument lost.

5. **pt · "outro" is left un-glossed and collides with an ordinary Portuguese word, in both places it
   appears.** — clause: *"**The gloss discipline — the most directly copyable thing here.** English
   terms kept and glossed in parentheses on first use"*.

   pt movement 3: *"três versos, refrão, ponte, outro"* — a Portuguese reader parses that as
   "bridge, another". pt movement 4: *"**O outro** repete um verso três vezes"* reads as "the other
   one repeats a verse three times", which is a sentence about a different object entirely, at the
   opening of the section the whole tense argument sits in. The draft applies the gloss discipline
   correctly to all three surviving English phrases and to `steering file`, `harness`, `hook`,
   `persona` and `blast radius`; this is the one place it lapses, and it lapses where the collision is
   total rather than merely unfamiliar.

   *Smallest change that clears it:* *"ponte, outro (o encerramento)"* on first use and *"O
   encerramento repete…"* on second. Two words.

- The pt and en editions were compared claim by claim: every figure, date and count matches

### Citable findings

1. **The article's headline count is contradicted by two of its own sections, and one of those
   contradictions is new in this round.** — clause: *"if you cannot point to where in the source
   material (an ADR, a `CLAUDE.md` passage, a transcript, a prior published piece, an explicit answer

   Movement 3 states the blanket: **"Every single one has a file, a bill, a commit or a measurement

   The close restates it as the takeaway: **"it lands fourteen times"** (`pt`: *"e ela acerta catorze
   vezes"*). Two sections in the audit itself say otherwise:

   - Section 14 says, in terms, **"It is the only image in the set with no mechanism under it."**
     That has contradicted movement 3 since `d2d2d48`; **I missed it in round 1 and it is mine, not a

     side: a plan that says nothing at all."** So the image's referent is adjacent, not identical —
     which is the honest thing to write, and it makes **"only"** in section 14 false as well.

   The source that disproves the claim is the article's own body. Nothing outside the printed text is
   needed to see it, so this is not a truth question routed to the merge gate — it is a claim the
   draft cannot point at a source for, in a piece whose entire subject is not reprinting claims you
   have not re-run. **This is the same defect the new README paragraph apologises for, one level up
   and still standing.**

   *What it costs the reader:* the reader who reaches section 5 or section 14 has been handed the
   counter-example to the sentence that sold them the article. On any other piece that is a slip; on
   this one it is the thesis failing against itself, and it is the first thing a hostile reader would
   quote back.

   *Smallest change that clears it:* say the real shape in movement 3 and let the close inherit it —
   *"Twelve have a file, a bill, a commit or a measurement behind them. One points at a fear I have
   from the other side, and one has nothing under it at all — and those two get their own sections,
   because they are the interesting ones."* Then **"it lands fourteen times"** in the close becomes
   *"it lands, and I can show you where each one lands or fails to"*, and section 14 drops **"only"**.
   Three edits, no material added, and the piece gets stronger: a count with two named exceptions
   reads as an audit, a count with none reads as a pitch.

2. **`pt` · the trust-root section gives a Portuguese reader a second version of the paragraph
   instead of an aside — twice, and once three times over.** — clause: *"**The technical reader gets
   an aside rather than a second version of the paragraph**"*, from the gloss discipline the
   precedence section admits by name as a mechanic.

   Round 1 cleared the `pt` gloss pattern at three short phrases across a whole article, and that
   clearance does not transfer at this density. In one section a `pt` reader now meets three English

   - *"The blast radius of an `apply` must not include the authority to perform the next `apply`."*
     **O raio de destruição de um `apply` não pode incluir a autoridade de fazer o `apply` seguinte.**
     — a sentence printed twice, unmarked.
   - *"If the provider's thumbprint or the infra role's trust policy is edited in the console, no
     `plan` objects."* **Se a impressão digital do provedor … nenhum `plan` reclama.** — then the very
     next sentence says it a **third** time: *"A ferramenta … é estruturalmente cega justo para o
     pedaço que dá acesso ao resto."*

   The corpus mechanic is a **term** glossed in parentheses, and he **announces** the move rather than
   hiding it. Neither happens here. The result is not a bilingual courtesy, it is a document being
   cited at a reader.

   *What it costs the reader:* the `pt` reader reads the section's two load-bearing propositions two
   and three times respectively, which is the one place in either edition where the prose stops

   line, because its whole point is that the record's own words are this article's title — and gloss

   keep only the Portuguese; the sentence after it already carries the meaning, so cut one of those

   *`en` is milder and is not raised as a finding:* it quotes the same three fragments with no
   translation, and only the blind-spot quotation is followed by a restatement of itself. One of those
