The yellow badge — AWS's five-year tenure badge — landed on my desk this month.

Here's the part worth saying before anything else, because it's the whole reason this is worth a post instead of a photo: **I actually hit five years in January 2026.** The badge isn't marking an anniversary. It's arriving about six months after one. AWS only made the badge available in Brazil recently — the object caught up with the milestone, not the other way around.

I could have written the easier version of this — "five years at AWS today" — and it would have read fine, right up until someone checked the math against my own LinkedIn timeline and found it off by half a year. The honest version is better anyway: a marker that shows up late is a more interesting thing to write about than a round number, and it doesn't ask you to trust an anniversary I'm not actually standing on today.

## What the five and a half years were, checkably

No client names — that's a standing rule here, and AWS Professional Services work runs entirely inside other companies' systems — but the shape of it is public and dated:

- **January 2021 – March 2023, Cloud Application Architect.** Embedded inside a tier-1 bank's engineering team, standing up the cloud-native foundation of its mortgage-credit domain — migrating it off mainframe and ASPX onto a modern full-stack platform. Grew from hands-on IC to tech lead of a cross-organization team of up to eight, over about two years.
- **April 2023 – present, Senior Delivery Consultant, App Modernization.** Tech lead standing up an oil & gas operator's AWS landing zone and delivering its first modernized workload, on an engagement the client renewed on the strength of the deliveries. Also architected a custom cloud-native replacement for a SaaS streaming platform — native apps across five platforms plus web, on a BFF-and-microservices backend. Along the way, in 2026, adopted AI-native development — Claude Code, Kiro, the practices this site is built on — inside that same delivery work, not separately from it.

Two roles, one throughline underneath both: distributed systems, delivered inside constraints — legacy, compliance, data sovereignty — that a green-field project never has to negotiate.

## Why this is the harder half to show

Everything above this line is checkable against a timeline — dates, titles, org charts. It doesn't explain why I'm spending weekends now building an agent harness instead of just doing more of what the badge already certifies I'm good at.

Here's the honest version, and it's smaller than a case study: I had an aspiration to master this — not "AI" as a category, the actual agentic, AI-native way of building software this site is about — while I was still at AWS. That aspiration ran alongside the work, not after it ended. 2026 is when it became visible in the deliveries themselves — Claude Code and Kiro, inside the same oil & gas engagement, not bolted onto it after hours. But the wanting predates that. It was there while the badge was still six months from existing.

Which is the actual reason the agentic work reads as pursuit rather than pivot: the five and a half years weren't time spent instead of chasing this. They were the years the aspiration was live in — the ones that gave it something to be tested against, in systems where constraints (legacy, compliance, data sovereignty) don't forgive a shortcut. What's next isn't a break from that. It's what the aspiration was pointing at the whole time.
